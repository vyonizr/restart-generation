# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

People who own Reset Generation (RedLynx, 2008) and want to play it again, alone or online. They have their own copy of the Windows standalone build, as `ResetGeneration-Standalone.zip` or the extracted folder, and either a desktop browser with a keyboard, or a phone or tablet that opens a public server's address or is on the same network as a PC that hosts the game. The page speaks to players only; hosting a server is covered in [Run a server](docs/HOSTING.md), not on the page.

## Product Purpose

The browser client runs the original game engine, unchanged, in an x86 emulator in the browser, and connects it to a replacement for the shut-down N-Gage Arena. Success means a player who owns the game goes from opening the page to playing in a few steps, and can play online against other people again.

## Positioning

The game itself, not a remake or a port: the original engine runs unmodified, and a replacement Arena server restores login, stats, rankings, achievements, friends, ranked matches through **Quick Match** (two to four players), friendly rooms, and battles. The page carries none of RedLynx's files; each player brings their own copy ([architecture decision record (ADR) 0001](docs/adr/0001-players-bring-their-own-game-files.md)).

## Operating Context

The page is a single static page, built from `web/` and `shared/` into `web/dist` ([ADR 0013](docs/adr/0013-the-page-is-built-with-vite-and-svelte.md), [ADR 0035](docs/adr/0035-the-page-lives-in-web-src-and-code-node-shares-lives-in-shared.md)), that is served in one of these ways:

- **By a game server that sends the game:** a server that isn't `--public`, with its server copy in `RG_GAME`, sends it to every visitor; a `--public` one only to a visitor whose link carries its download code ([ADR 0031](docs/adr/0031-the-server-sends-its-copy-only-to-a-download-code.md)). The page keeps the files in the browser's IndexedDB, and the player goes straight to **Start**. Nothing on the page shows or asks for the code.
- **By a static host, or a game server that doesn't send the game to this visitor:** each player first chooses their own `.zip` or folder. The page keeps the files in the browser's IndexedDB, so a player chooses them once per browser, and the files never leave the player's computer.

The page moves through these states: loading the files, choosing the files, **Start** (or the rejoin card in its place), starting the engine, playing, the dialogs described in the following paragraphs, and errors (missing files, unreadable files, not enough memory, the emulator stopping).

Online play depends on the server:

- **With Discord sign-in ([ADR 0011](docs/adr/0011-guests-and-members-with-discord-sign-in.md)):** the page makes a guest on the first visit, keeps its name and a secret token in local storage, and logs the engine in with no form. The header, or the who chip at the top of the page on a touch device, shows the name, **Replays**, and **Sign in with Discord**; a member sees an **Account** menu with **Replays**, **Download my data**, **Sign out**, and **Delete my account**. When the player comes back from Discord, a dialog asks a new member to choose a name. When that Discord account already has a player, a dialog asks the player to confirm that their guest progress and this browser's saved game are discarded. After a guest earns fame, a one-line offer to sign in appears under the screen, at most once a day.
- **Without accounts (local, LAN):** the Start screen says "Online as" and the player's name from the first visit, a random `GuestNNNNNN` until they choose one: **Set name**, or **Change name** once a name is set, opens the name dialog there and then, and the name dialog also appears at the first online login if no name is set by then. On a touch device the who chip at the top of the page shows the name instead, with **Set name** or **Change name** in its menu, during play too. The name is kept in local storage with no password ([ADR 0009](docs/adr/0009-a-name-is-the-whole-identity.md)); a new name starts with fresh stats, or takes over the stats of an existing name. Settings and achievements are also kept in local storage.

When a server answers, the page shows how many players are online: in the header's right end on a desktop, and at the top of the page, across from the who chip, on a touch device, where it stays while the game runs.

A player whose page lost its place in a running battle, by a reload, a crash, or a phone asleep too long, has two minutes to come back: the server holds their seat as an [absent player](CONTEXT.md)'s ([ADR 0038](docs/adr/0038-a-battle-seat-outlives-its-session.md)). The battle goes on meanwhile, and the others see that player's phases end when their time limit runs out, as a player's who does nothing would ([ADR 0040](docs/adr/0040-an-absent-player-is-timed-out-at-the-phase-time-limit.md)). Before **Start**, the page asks the server about the seat with its login's own credentials, and a card takes **Start**'s place: the kind of match, the seats with the player's own marked, the time left as a meter and a clock, a large **Rejoin**, and **Leave** under it. **Rejoin** starts the game and presses through the menus to Quick Match and a hero by itself, listing the steps as they pass, while the player's input waits; the engine replays the battle so far without its animations and lands in it at the current turn ("Back in"), and the player plays on from the next phase. If that hasn't worked 45 seconds after **Rejoin**, a strip at the foot of the screen shows Arena → Quick Match → Hero, the clock, and **Leave**, and the player does it by hand. **Leave** gives the seat up at once, as leaving through the game's menus does, and the battle counts as a defeat. A seat whose window ran out while the page was closed shows "Dropped out" once, and **OK** goes on to **Start**.

Once the copy has loaded, **Guide** opens the [guide](CONTEXT.md), the game's own how-to-play, heroes and items from its info menu, which newcomers rarely find: under the screen at the left end on a desktop, and as a book in the touch pad. The page reads it from the player's copy in the browser, in the game's language and with the player's keys, and keeps it only in memory; a visitor without a copy has none, and a copy whose info menu can't be read shows no **Guide** ([ADR 0033](docs/adr/0033-the-guide-reads-the-info-menu-from-the-players-copy.md)).

A public server also serves two reading pages, **Terms** (`/terms`) and the **Privacy policy** (`/privacy`), filled in with its admin, its hosting provider, and its contact ([ADR 0021](docs/adr/0021-public-servers-publish-terms-and-a-privacy-policy.md)). Playing online there accepts both; nothing blocks play to ask.

On a public server whose admin turns them on (`RG_STATS=1`), the server keeps **visitor statistics** ([CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md), [ADR 0043](docs/adr/0043-visitor-statistics-are-counted-by-the-gateway-itself.md)): each of the four pages, the game, a replay, the Terms and the Privacy policy, tells the server once it opens which kind it is, the referring site, and whether it runs on a touch device, and the server adds the country and counts each day's unique visitors without writing any IP address down. Only the admin sees the counts, with `npm run stats` on the server. The Privacy policy then describes them and offers **Don't count my visits**, remembered in the browser; a browser that sends Global Privacy Control is never counted. Everywhere else nothing is counted.

After each online battle, the notice line under the screen says the battle is saved as a **replay** ([CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md)) and offers **Watch replay**, which opens the replay page in a new tab, and **Copy link**; it stays until the player dismisses it or their next battle starts. The replay page (`/replay/<id>`) plays the battle again in the engine's own replay player, as the original website did, at 420 × 378: the battle view as a broadcast replay, with a title band over it (a crimson **REPLAY** tag, the day and the kind of match, **Share** and **Download replay**), the seats under it as a lower third (seat in its color in the battle, name and hero; a [defeated player](CONTEXT.md)'s seat turns hollow once the game has shown the defeat, and once the replay ends each winner's seat wears a crown), then our own control strip (play or pause, a time slider with the time, **Skip idle**, **2×**, mute and volume). A click or tap on the battle plays or pauses it; Space, the arrow keys, F and M work too, and a gamepad's bottom button and D-pad. The end screen dims the battle and gives the result and **Watch again**. The page gets the game files as the game page does, and asks for **Watch** before it plays, so the sound can start. Each player keeps the replays of their last 25 battles, and a replay lasts until it is in none of its players' last 25 ([ADR 0034](docs/adr/0034-each-player-keeps-their-last-25-replays.md)); after that, or on a server with replays off, the page says it's gone. A pasted link shows a text-only preview: players, heroes, kind of match, environment and day.

A replay whose battle the engine hasn't built after 10 seconds of its own time, such as damaged data or a development seed's placeholder, never will: the page says **Can't play**, with **Play the game**, instead of staying black.

A replay the server's witness followed has [marks](CONTEXT.md) on its time slider: one tick for each turn in which a princess is rescued or a hero is knocked out, set at the start of that turn so the move and its animation play after a jump ([ADR 0030](docs/adr/0030-replay-marks-are-worked-out-by-the-witness-when-the-replay-is-saved.md)). Each tick names its moments, such as "Ada rescues Bo's princess", and a click goes to it; **Previous moment** and **Next moment** beside play, Shift with ← or →, and a gamepad's shoulder buttons step through them. A replay without marks, saved before them or by a server without a server copy, keeps a plain slider.

**Share**, in the replay page's title band, opens a phone's or tablet's share sheet with the page's address, and copies the address elsewhere. **Download replay**, beside it, opens a menu of **With Details** and **Battle Only** and saves the replay as [footage](CONTEXT.md) of the kind picked: an MP4 file to cut in a video editor and share, with the game's sound effects but not its soundtrack ([ADR 0028](docs/adr/0028-players-can-export-a-replay-as-footage.md), [ADR 0041](docs/adr/0041-footage-and-showcase-clips-leave-out-the-soundtrack.md)). **With Details**, [broadcast footage](CONTEXT.md), comes first and takes focus every time; nothing remembers the pick. The page renders it on its own engine, faster than real time, with a progress bar and **Cancel** over the battle view: the whole battle at the page's **2×** and **Skip idle** as they are when it is pressed (a file at 2× is named with `-2x`), the engine's picture three times its size in the [screen finish](CONTEXT.md) the page shows (smoothed under its pixel grid with **Soft screen** on, square pixels with it off), at 30 frames a second, with its sound, and one more second of its end. **With Details** adds the title band over the battle (the REPLAY tag, the day and the kind of match) and the lower third under it, always in one row, with its defeated seats and, at the end, its winners' crowns as the page shows them; it holds the end for 3 seconds, and its file name ends in `-details`. Then the replay waits at the start for **Play**. It needs a browser that encodes H.264 and AAC (WebCodecs), on a secure page: over HTTPS, or at localhost. On a LAN address over plain HTTP the button stays disabled and the page says to open the replay over HTTPS or at localhost; in a browser that can't encode, it says to try Chrome or Edge.

**Replays** lists the player's last 25 battles under their name, newest first, for anyone to see ([ADR 0024](docs/adr/0024-a-players-replays-are-listed-under-their-name.md), [ADR 0034](docs/adr/0034-each-player-keeps-their-last-25-replays.md)), so a dismissed notice loses nothing. On a Discord server it sits in a member's **Account** menu and beside a guest's **Sign in with Discord**; on a server without accounts, on the "Online as" line under **Start**; on a touch device, in the who chip's menu. It opens the **Replays** dialog: one line per battle with its date, a Ranked or Friendly icon, a small sketch of its environment, the hero the player played, whether they won, and **Watch** and **Copy link**. On a touch device the who chip keeps it a tap away while the game runs.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Runs only the Windows standalone build. A player who owns only the Mac copy can't play.
- Runs in any desktop browser on any operating system. On a keyboard, arrow keys move, Ctrl or Enter selects, Shift goes back, and Esc opens the menu. A keyboard works on every device, including a tablet with one attached. Those are the default keys. A player changes them in the game's own Custom Keys screen, and the page's key legend and touch pad follow their choice (see [custom keys](CONTEXT.md)).
- Phones and tablets (touch devices) play on a public server over HTTPS, or over the LAN from a PC that runs the server with `--lan`, and open the page in Android Chrome or in Safari on iOS and iPadOS. The game plays with a touch pad: in portrait under the game screen, a round D-pad with **Select** and **Back** to its right; in landscape the D-pad left of the screen and the buttons right of it. Under them a labeled bar holds **Soft screen** to switch the screen finish, **Keyboard** for typing, **Full board** to switch the board view, **Fullscreen** where the browser supports it, and **Guide** when the copy has a guide. Online modes work as on desktop. As of October 2026, the touch layout has been tested only in emulation, not on a real device.
- A **gamepad** ([CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md)) works on desktops and touch devices, on any page address: the D-pad or the left stick moves, the bottom button selects, the right button goes back, and Start opens the menu. Buttons go by position, so on a Nintendo pad the button labeled B selects. It also presses **Start** and answers the CPU players box and the rejoin card. The page uses pads with the standard layout, and on Android also the ones Chrome reports with no layout name; it ignores others. Only one pad is used at a time, it never vibrates, and it has no chat button and no remapping. As of October 2026, nobody has played the page with a real gamepad.
- On a desktop, the **pointer** ([CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md)) plays a battle's tile placement, hero turns, and cannons on the battlefield, beside the keys and a gamepad ([pointer play](CONTEXT.md), [ADR 0039](docs/adr/0039-pointer-play-turns-clicks-into-the-engines-own-cursor-moves.md)). The page moves the engine's own cursor and presses Select, so everything sent online is what keys would have sent. On the Full board the cursor follows a mouse or a pen, a hero's route follows the pointer's trail, and a click on the cursor's cell commits; a click elsewhere only moves the cursor there. On Classic, and with a finger on a touchscreen laptop, a click moves the cursor and a second click on the same cell commits. A click drops a piece, with no button to hold; the right button turns the piece, and in a hero's turn takes the cursor back to the hero. A release over another cell than the press cancels it. In other players' turns, and after the player is defeated, the pointer only moves the cursor, by hover on the Full board and by clicks on Classic, and the engine shows the [reach area](CONTEXT.md) of the hero under it: nothing is committed or sent, and the right button does nothing. The engine's menus, the battle menu, and chat stay on the keys, the corner panels take no clicks, and the page draws nothing of its own. A copy without the engine bytes pointer play needs gets none. A touch device keeps the touch pad.
- Every story and hero is open from the start only on a server that isn't `--public` ([ADR 0007](docs/adr/0007-patch-the-engine-in-memory.md)); elsewhere they are earned. A member's saved game (story progress, settings, and achievements) is kept on the server, so it follows them across browsers; a guest's stays in one browser ([ADR 0014](docs/adr/0014-a-members-saved-game-is-kept-on-the-server.md)).
- The game screen is drawn by the engine: at 240 × 320 on the phone's screen, and in a battle on the **Full board** ([board view](CONTEXT.md), the default on a desktop) at 420 × 378, the size of the original website's replay player, so the whole battlefield is on screen ([ADR 0017](docs/adr/0017-a-battle-gets-the-full-board-through-engine-hooks.md)). There, screens laid out for the phone show at 240 × 320 inside the engine's own [curtain](CONTEXT.md). **Classic**, the default on a phone or tablet, where a 420-pixel-wide board would come out smaller than the engine drew it, keeps every battle on the phone's screen. A change applies at once, in a battle too. The page frames the engine's screen and never redraws it. By default, the soft [screen finish](CONTEXT.md) smooths how the screen is shown. The player can switch to sharp, and the engine's output is never altered.
- The page is built with Vite and Svelte ([ADR 0013](docs/adr/0013-the-page-is-built-with-vite-and-svelte.md)). The server, the emulator, and the other modules Node imports stay plain JavaScript with no build step and no dependencies.
- The project's author runs a public server. The page is the same on every server and names none of them.
- Undecided: whether most players arrive through a static host or a server that serves the files. The page must handle both equally.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: Restart Generation. The page is an unofficial fan revival of Reset Generation (RedLynx, 2008); the game keeps its own name.
- Required notices: the page shows each of the following, word for word where quoted:
  - **Footer line, always:** "Unofficial fan revival of Reset Generation © 2008 RedLynx / Nokia; not affiliated with RedLynx, Ubisoft or Nokia." and a credit to vyonizr.
  - **Notices dialog:** the footer's **Notices** button opens a dialog over the page. The dialog never opens by itself.
  - **Notices dialog, always:** "Restart Generation is an unofficial fan revival of Reset Generation © 2008 RedLynx / Nokia. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by RedLynx, Ubisoft or Nokia, and their names and trademarks belong to their owners."
  - **Notices dialog, where the player brings the files:** the note "Windows standalone build only. Your files stay in this browser."
  - **Notices dialog, where a server sends the game files itself:** a statement that it does, with the admin's `RG_CONTACT` for a rights holder who wants them removed ([ADR 0018](docs/adr/0018-publishing-with-legal-guardrails.md)). On a public server only a browser that received them for the download code sees it, as "This server sent you a copy" ([ADR 0031](docs/adr/0031-the-server-sends-its-copy-only-to-a-download-code.md)).
  - **Footer line, on a public server:** links to its **Terms** and **Privacy policy**.
  - **Under Start, on a public server:** "By playing online you accept the Terms and the Privacy policy", with both linked. The name dialog for a new member says the same of saving the name.
  - **Notices dialog, on a public server:** links to its Terms and Privacy policy, which say what it keeps ([ADR 0021](docs/adr/0021-public-servers-publish-terms-and-a-privacy-policy.md)).
  - **Notices dialog, always:** that the project is free software under the GNU General Public License, version 3, with no warranty, linking to `/licenses.txt` and to the [source repository](https://github.com/vyonizr/restart-generation). On a server, the same line ends with the version it runs, such as "Version 0.1.2".
- No RedLynx, Ubisoft or Nokia logos, and nothing that reads as an official RedLynx, Ubisoft or Nokia site.

## Evidence on Hand

- No screenshots, art, or other assets derived from the game ship with the page or the repository ([ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-players-bring-their-own-game-files.md)). The only game imagery is the engine's own screen and the guide's pictures, which the page reads from the player's files after they load.
- Repository: [vyonizr/restart-generation](https://github.com/vyonizr/restart-generation), linked from the notices dialog.
- No player counts beyond the live online count, no testimonials, and no press. Don't invent them.

## Product Principles

1. The game leads. Everything around it helps a player reach **Start** and then gets out of the way.
2. The player's files stay theirs. Say plainly that the files stay in the browser, and never ship or imply anything derived from the game.
3. Honest about status. Say what works; don't claim official backing, a server the page isn't on, or support for the Mac build.
4. One page for both hosting modes. Choosing files and going straight to **Start** are both first-class paths.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The game plays with a keyboard, with the touch pad on phones and tablets, or with a gamepad on either, so a player without a keyboard can play too, on a public server or on a LAN that a PC hosts. On a desktop the pointer plays a battle too, with clicks alone: nothing needs a held button or a drag. The touch pad's controls are real buttons with names for screen readers, and the gamepad version of the key legend names each button by its position and by its label on Xbox, PlayStation, and Nintendo pads. The rest of the page still scrolls and zooms on touch devices. If a device runs the game too slowly or lacks the memory for it, the page says so plainly.
